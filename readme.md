# When radar waveform hides a picture: the mystery of semantic stealth transmission in ISAC systems [with Python code]

The article discusses the new challenges and opportunities in ISAC (Integrated Sensing and Communication) systems, which aim to integrate communication and perception. The main focus is on how to ensure that while a radar waveform carries information, it remains undetected by potential listeners.

One of the key challenges is that radar and communication signals share the same waveform, which means they both have dual roles: detecting targets and transmitting information. This poses a problem because if a third party were to analyze this "abnormal" waveform, they might be able to detect the presence of communication content.

For example, imagine a sentry patrolling at night with a steady beam from his flashlight. If he starts using the flashlight's flickering to send messages, even if the flicker is very fast and faint, an alert opponent could still notice something unusual about the light.

This is the core contradiction that the authors of the paper are trying to solve: How can we ensure that the presence of communication remains hidden from potential "guardians" (Wardens) in an open wireless uplink without compromising the accuracy of radar perception or the semantic fidelity of communication content?
Two. "Three Difficulties": Hiddenness, Perception Accuracy, and Semantic Quality Jointly Guaranteed
Traditional concealed communication largely focuses on the dimension of "whether there is a signal being transmitted," striving for low interception probability, essentially manipulating at the power spectrum level. However, in the context of ISAC, the signal is inherently present—radars are designed to transmit waveforms to detect targets. Therefore, the key issue no longer lies in whether there is a signal; what truly needs to be hidden is: whether this waveform has additionally loaded communication semantics.
Here arises a previously unsystematically addressed "three difficult dilemmas" within ISAC:

1. Hiddenness: The guardian must discern whether the waveform differs from purely perceptual patterns through statistical detection, and the system must ensure that the two types of waveforms are statistically indistinguishable.
2. Perception fidelity: After embedded communication information, the radar's matched filter response must not degrade, otherwise, estimates of target distance, velocity, and angle will drift.
3. Semantic accuracy: The semantic information (including scene images, target statuses, etc.) compressed into the waveform must be reconstructed with high quality at the receiving end.
CoSMIC (Concealedness-oriented Semantic Modulation in ISAC Chirps) was specifically designed to solve these "three difficult dilemmas."

Design Philosophy of CoSMIC: Let Semantics "Long" Within Radar Waveforms
CoSMIC's approach is very clever: it does not construct an "invisible channel" separately but directly utilizes the information generated by radar perception as the semantic content for the next frame of communication.
The system operates on frames, with each frame's detection results—including the image of the target scene, distance, velocity, angle, and category—being encoded and embedded into the next frame's dual-function linear frequency modulated waveform. In other words, what the radar "sees" precisely is what it transmits. This closed loop mechanism of "perceiving first, then transmitting" establishes a natural causal connection between semantics and waveforms, rather than merely superimposing a layer of communication load.
From a scenario perspective, the system contains three roles:
Alice: A dual-function transmitter that not only performs radar detection but also embeds the sensed output into waveform transmission.
Bob: A legitimate receiver, sharing a set of pseudorandom reference sequences with Alice, capable of demodulating semantic information.
Willie: A potential guardian, attempting to determine whether the waveform transmitted by Alice contains communication content through statistical detection.
Willie faces a binary hypothesis test: Is the radar signal purely a perception waveform (H₀) or a double waveform containing semantic information (H₁).
IV. Semantic Rotation Coding: "Twisting" an image into Gaussian noise - The core coding strategy of CoSMIC is called semantic rotation coding, which is elegant in its simplicity: the transmitted waveform maintains constant modality regardless of whether it embeds semantic information, with the envelope and radiation power being consistent.
Specifically, each transmission frame is organized into several pairs of shared Gaussian reference vectors. Alice and Bob independently generate identical reference sequences through prearranged seeds, forming their "codebook." The semantic encoder (based on the ConvNeXt architecture) maps the input perception image and target state onto the rotation angles and scaling factors on each pair of reference vectors. Semantic information is not directly attached to the waveform; instead, it is written through the paired rotation transformation of the reference vectors.
If you imagine a unit vector forming a point on a plane, then rotation operation is about rotating this point around the origin—the direction of rotation corresponds to the semantic meaning. Scaling operation changes the distance of this point from the origin.
The key lies in: In pure perception mode, the reference vector does not undergo any transformation, equivalent to zero rotation angle and scaling factor of one. In semantic mode, the vector undergoes rotation and scaling. However, since both modes use the same Gaussian distribution for their reference vectors, from the perspective of external observers, the statistical distribution of both modes merely depends on the deviation from the scaling factor and one, irrespective of the rotation angle.
5. A Graceful Mathematical Guarantee: Ensuring Any Listener Can Only "Guess"
The paper presents a very powerful theoretical result: the Kullback-Leibler divergence (KL-divergence, a core metric for measuring the difference between two distributions), which is scaled by a deviation, can be bounded in closed form. 
In other words, as long as the system ensures that the total KL-divergence formed by the scaling factors in each frame does not exceed a preset threshold (controlled by parameter ε, up to a maximum of 2ε²), then regardless of whether Willie uses a traditional energy detector or a deep learning-based classifier, his optimal detection error probability will not be lower than 1 - ε. When ε is set to 0.10, Willie's overall detection error rate remains above 90%, almost equivalent to random guessing.
More importantly, this constraint is differentiable. The system gradually shrinks the original scaling factors towards 1 through a simple binary search process during forward propagation until it satisfies the concealment budget. This entire process can be seamlessly integrated into end-to-end training without the need for additional adversarial training or game-theoretic optimization.
This is equivalent to giving the system a "peephole" at the physical layer: before the guard sees what the system has calculated, the system has already constrained the signal within that limit.
The paper provides solid theoretical analysis on this. Under semantic embedding, CoSMIC's waveforms undergo matched filtering processing, and the main lobe response remains unchanged - meaning that radar's peak position and resolution on the distance-Doppler plane will not degrade due to semantic embedding. The change in output signal-to-noise ratio is also controllable and bounded.
Intuitive understanding: CoSMIC's encoding operation acts on the "relative relationship" between Gaussian reference pairs, while matched filtering mainly relies on the self-correlation properties of the waveform. Since the transmitted waveform always maintains a constant modulus, the basic structure of pulse compression is preserved. This allows the radar's perceptual function to remain stable and reliable even after embedding semantic information.
VII. The Magic of the Receiver: Recovering Semantics from Noise
If the transmitter is hiding semantics, then the receiver is searching for them—and it's doing so from waveforms that have been subjected to both wireless channel fading and noise pollution.
Bob's receiver process involves a series of delicate inverse operations: first, it compensates for channel fading through minimum mean square error equalization, then removes the linear frequency modulation base, followed by nonlinear phase reconstitution to restore the signal's real-valued representation, then reverses the autoregressive filtering to approximate the reference vector, and finally performs pairwise least square projection on each pair of reference sequences—the result is a rough semantic latent variable.
This rough latent variable is not a Gaussian white noise with isotropic distortion. It has a clear directionality (since semantics are embedded in the angles of the vectors), and is also affected by errors in channel equalization, nonlinear phase reconstitution, and the multiple effects of the transmitter's scaling and shrinkage.
Traditional Gaussian denoising methods smooth out the angle structure—which is precisely where the key carriers of semantics lie.
CoSMIC's solution introduces a reliability-guided integrator. Unlike iterative diffusion models that require dozens or hundreds of steps for sampling, this integrator advances the rough latent variables along a straight "bridge" towards clean latent variables, requiring only two steps to complete the process. The core advantage of the integrator lies in its learning speed field during training, which it aims to be a direct bridge, while diffusion models need to traverse bent random paths.
What's more interesting is the "reliability-guided" mechanism. At the receiver, it calculates a series of reliability features—including reference pair energy, estimated amplitude, received SNR proxy, and phase residuals—indicating to the integrator which parts of the signal are trustworthy and which need more correction. This targeted guidance strategy allows the integrator to significantly improve the final semantic reconstruction quality without increasing any error bounds.
Reconstruction quality significantly surpasses the diffusion model scheme. Under the same strict concealment constraints (ε=0.10), CoSMIC's semantic reconstruction PSNR has improved by about 18% to 20% compared to the diffusion-aided method. After integrating a diode flow refiner, the contour and detail recovery of targets in scene images are clearer, and the estimation errors for target distance, speed, angle, and category have also significantly decreased.
The inference speed is significantly ahead. The overall inference pipeline with the ConvNeXt encoder plus two steps of diode flow achieves several times faster than the diffusion model that requires dozens of steps of sampling, which is of significant importance for the real deployment of ISAC scenarios in real time.
The concealment is fully verified. The detection error on Willie side remains close to random guessing under different SNR levels, indicating that CoSMIC indeed achieves indistinguishability from a statistical perspective, not merely "appearing hard to detect".
From theory to practice: how far can this path go? From a more macro perspective, the value of CoSMIC extends beyond proposing a specific concealed communication scheme; it establishes a complete framework that elevates the issue of concealment in ISAC from an engineering intuition to a mathematical problem that can be theoretically derived and end-to-end optimized.
Three safeguards—closed KL divergence constraint, perceptual performance preservation, and efficient diode flow reconstruction—form the pillars of this framework, providing the basic conditions for moving from simulation to actual deployment.
Of course, there are still many challenges to overcome from the laboratory to practical implementation. Whether channel modeling covers more complex multipath environments? How can the semantic capacity be further expanded in multi-target scenarios? If guardians use more complex multi-frame joint detection strategies, is the CLD upper bound still tight enough? The answers to these questions will determine the speed of technological maturity.
However, CoSMIC has shown us a new possibility: in the future smart networks, communication is not just "faster and more" but can also be "safer and more covert." Every time radar pulses through space, it carries not only information about target echoes but also a silent scene scroll—perhaps this is the romantic charm of the next generation of integrated perception and communication.
